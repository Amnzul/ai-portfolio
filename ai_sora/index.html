Experimenting with AI Video Using Sora

Personal Exploration of Generative Video — 2025

Receiving Early Access

In 2025, I was excited to receive an invitation to test Sora, OpenAI's generative video model. Having previously experimented with AI-generated images, this felt like the next natural step in my exploration of generative AI. Instead of creating still artwork, I could now experiment with bringing ideas to life through motion.

My goal wasn't to produce professional films. I simply wanted to explore the technology, understand its capabilities, and have fun seeing what creative ideas it could bring to life.

Bringing My Cats to Life

Some of my favorite experiments involved my own cats. Using photographs that I had taken, I explored Sora's image-to-video feature to create humorous animated clips. I imagined them talking, reacting to different situations, and starring in playful fantasy adventures that would have been impossible to film traditionally.

These projects were created purely for entertainment, but they highlighted how accessible AI video generation had become. I shared a few of my favorite clips with family and friends, although most of my experiments were simply for my own enjoyment.

Watching a still photograph transform into a moving scene was one of the most enjoyable aspects of using Sora. It made familiar images feel entirely new.

Exploring Fantasy Worlds

Beyond the lighthearted cat videos, I also spent time creating cinematic high-fantasy scenes. I experimented with dramatic environments, magical landscapes, and imaginative visual storytelling to see how well Sora could interpret descriptive prompts.

One feature I particularly enjoyed was remixing. Being able to iterate on existing videos made experimentation fast and enjoyable, allowing me to refine ideas without starting from scratch every time. The overall ease of use encouraged creative exploration rather than technical complexity.

Challenges and Lessons

Although Sora was capable of producing impressive results, I learned that creating exactly the video I envisioned often required patience. The model generally understood the intent of my prompts, but the final output did not always match the scene I had imagined. Producing satisfying results usually meant generating multiple variations and refining prompts through several iterations.

This experience reinforced a lesson I had already encountered with AI image generation: the technology is remarkably capable, but successful creative work still depends on experimentation, iteration, and clear communication with the model.

Looking Ahead

After experimenting with Sora, I became even more optimistic about the future of AI-assisted filmmaking. As models continue improving, I believe they will eventually be able to generate longer, more coherent scenes while maintaining consistent characters and storytelling.

If that happens, AI video generation could become a powerful creative tool for independent creators and fan communities, making it possible to produce high-quality films and visual stories that would previously have required much larger budgets and production teams.

My time with Sora was both educational and entertaining. It expanded my understanding of generative AI beyond images and demonstrated how rapidly AI video technology is evolving.
